Awareness isn't enough — meaning needs a verb.

My first idea said the meaning of life is that consciousness exists. The universe waking up and looking at itself. That's a cool image. But it's **incomplete**. A mirror reflecting itself forever doesn't do anything. It just... sits there.

Here's what bugged me: if awareness alone is the meaning, then a person in a coma who still has brain activity is living just as meaningfully as someone building a hospital or raising a kid. That can't be right. Something is **missing**.

The missing piece is **doing**. Consciousness that just exists is like a light with no one in the room. Meaning shows up when awareness **reaches toward** something. A dog learns your name. A kid asks why the sky is blue. A stranger holds a door open. These aren't just awareness — they're awareness pointed outward, connecting to something beyond itself.

So the meaning isn't that the universe can ask questions. It's that it **tries to answer them**. The asking without the trying is empty. A question that nobody chases is just noise.

This also helps with the suffering problem. Suffering isn't meaningful just because you're conscious of it. It becomes meaningful when you **respond** to it — when you push back, learn, help someone else avoid it.

Meaning needs a direction, not just a spotlight.
